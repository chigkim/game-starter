# game-starter

Make small games for your young child by describing them in plain words. An AI coding agent, such as Claude Code, Codex or Gemini CLI, builds the game for you. The games play in a web browser on phones and tablets, including iPad.

## Start a game

Open your AI coding agent in an empty folder, then paste this, with your own idea at the end:

```text
Look at https://github.com/chigkim/game-starter and make <your idea>
```

For example: "Look at https://github.com/chigkim/game-starter and make a counting game for my 4-year-old who loves trucks."

The agent asks you a few questions, builds the game, and can put it online so your child can play it on a tablet.

For your next game or a change, open the agent in the same folder and say what you want.

## Ideas to paste

- a counting game for my 4-year-old who loves trucks. Trucks drive in with blocks, and she taps to count them.
- a letter-sound game for my 5-year-old. A friendly owl says a sound, and he taps the matching letter. He can't read yet, so everything should be spoken.
- a dress-up game where my 3-year-old picks hats, shirts and shoes for a puppy. No reading, no losing, lots of silly animations.
- a gentle space game for my 6-year-old. Steer a little rocket with a finger, collect stars and visit planets. Nothing scary and no game over.
- a shape-sorting game for my 2-year-old with big shapes to drag into holes, bright colors and happy sounds.
- a times-tables practice game for my 8-year-old who loves dinosaurs. She earns dinosaur stickers and her progress is saved.

The more you say about your child, the better the game fits: their age, whether they can read, what they love right now, and anything to avoid.
